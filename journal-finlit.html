SYSTEMS DESIGN · FINLIT

Turning money decisions into a life simulation

A field note by Ishani · about 2 min read

FinLit is a mobile financial-literacy RPG. Its described systems connect work and salary to everyday needs, health, housing, debt, investing, family events, and long-term retirement goals. That combination interests me because it treats money as part of a life rather than as a spreadsheet detached from consequences.

The hard design problem is not adding more financial features. It is making the cause and effect readable. If the player takes a job, spends on groceries, advances a month, or buys a home, the game should make clear what changed and why. Otherwise complexity feels arbitrary, and advice arrives too late to help.

Make consequences teachable

A useful simulation can let players explore trade-offs without pretending there is one perfect answer. Saving may protect against a surprise expense but delay a home purchase. A higher-paying job may come with a cost to energy or health. A month-end explanation can connect the result to the choice that produced it. The educational value is in that visible relationship, not in simply presenting financial vocabulary.

For each system, I would test one question at a time: Can a player explain why their cash changed this month? Do they know which needs are urgent? Does a life event create a decision, or only a penalty? If a recommendation appears, can they see which part of their situation it responds to?

Design principle: A simulation teaches best when its rules are legible enough to reason about. If the player cannot connect an outcome to a choice, adding another lesson or tooltip will not fix the underlying model.
